## November on Jeju

In November I went down to Jeju on a business trip. They said snow had already fallen at the 1100 Highland, yet the weather below was strangely warm. It was a day when winter and autumn sat side by side on a single island.

Around lunchtime, between appointments, I stopped by the Ark Church in Andeok. It is a church designed by the Zainichi architect Itami Jun (Yu Dong-ryong) with Noah's Ark as its motif, completed in 2009. I had known its name for a long time, but this was the first time I stood in front of it.

## A Roof That Reflects the Light

The moment I stepped out of the car, the roof caught my eye first. Hundreds of triangular metal plates were sending sunlight back at their own different angles. Each time a cloud passed, the place of the glitter shifted, and the roof seemed to move even though it stood still.

Before that sight, a small awe, a radiance, and an unexpected relief came over me all at once. Was it because of the name, Ark? It felt like watching a ship that had passed through great waters and finally reached a safe place.

## A Building Afloat on Water

A shallow pool lies around the building. The closer I came, the more the church seemed to float not on the ground but on a small lake. The sky, the clouds, and the building's glass walls were reflected on the surface just as they were.

I sensed that the stillness there had not come about by chance. The water puts a single step of distance between the building and the person, and that distance lowers the voice and slows the walk. It was a stillness by intention.

![](img/bangju-church-3.jpg)

## Wood and Iron

The walls are a facade wrapped in long vertical timber, and an iron roof covers it. The wood holds the light, and the iron throws it back. Within one building, the ways of meeting light are opposite.

The wood, weathered gray by long wind and rain, joins naturally with the silver grass, the stone walls, and the yellowed dry lawn around it. The roof, on the other hand, resembles nothing around it. What belongs to the earth and what belongs to the sky are fitted together without a hair's gap, and this contrast gives the building its strength.

## The Gray Zone

I think a place of worship should be neither too close to the public nor too far from it. Anyone should be able to enter, but those who enter should feel they have come somewhere different from the outside. It is a gray zone somewhere in between.

Had the whole building been made from a single material, this gray area would have been hard to realize. Wood alone is too familiar, and iron alone is too cold. Iron and wood, and water and landscape. The combination of all these conveys the message of "embrace" and the atmosphere of "reverence" at once.

On the way back, the roof I looked back at was still shining. Beneath a distant, snow-covered Hallasan, it was a single ship resting quietly on the water, holding the sunlight of a warm lunchtime.
